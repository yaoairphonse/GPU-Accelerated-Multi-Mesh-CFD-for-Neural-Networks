Quick CFD development record · 24 October 2026

Bounded SA face operator and matched DP-LUR test

Final disposition: retain as an opt-in research scheme; reject promotion as the current CRM load-accuracy or load-convergence fix.

Decision. The method improves the SA residual at low cost and is numerically sound enough to keep behind --sa-face-scheme bounded. It does not change the low-frequency CL/CD/CM trajectory, misses the registered 2× residual gate, and is therefore not enabled by default. Cancel the proposal that SA face/implicit repair alone is the next remedy for the CRM load error.

Why this method was developed

The procedural Cartesian-AMR solver used cell-centered first-order SA convection, a corrected nonorthogonal diffusion gradient, and a scalar DP-LUR approximation based on the acoustic spectral radius. The nonlinear diffusion and implicit two-point coefficient could disagree, and a corrected gradient could be locally anti-diffusive. Earlier tetrahedral-grid experiments had already rejected merely adding relaxation sweeps or swapping one upwind coefficient, so this experiment changed the complete scalar face backbone instead of repeating those tests.

Implemented experimental operator

Convection

Monotone first-order SA transport was selected after a bounded second-order isolation produced a larger operator jump and a worse 150-update residual. Mean-flow convection remains second order.

Diffusion

The high-order normal gradient is blended with a positive two-point gradient. Its ratio to the two-point backbone is bounded to [0.1, 4], guaranteeing non-positive diffusion energy for every nonzero face jump.

Implicit action

DP-LUR uses the same cell-centered Rusanov mass-upwind coefficient for its diagonal and neighbor action, plus the same two-point diffusion backbone. Nonorthogonal correction remains deferred in the nonlinear residual.

The original conservative local pseudo-time bound was deliberately retained so the experiment did not obtain an apparent speedup merely by enlarging the time step. The legacy scheme remains the default and reproduced the previous build to roundoff.

Verification before the CRM comparison

  • Passed: CPU and CUDA builds and both CTests.
  • Passed: analytic limiter and per-face diffusion-dissipation tests, including wrong-sign and excessive nonorthogonal corrections.
  • Passed: 7,661,606-control CPU/CUDA uniform flow; maximum residual was about 4.93×10-13 and global imbalance below 6.62×10-14.
  • Passed: one DP-LUR update agreed between CPU and CUDA to global relative L∞ 1.87×10-16 and SA relative L∞ 1.80×10-16.
  • Passed: no invalid density, pressure, or SA state and no rejected update.

Rejected intermediate paths

  • Recomputing a full HLLC mass flux in every DP-LUR sweep produced nearly the same residual history but increased the 150-update process time from 70.7 s to 99.2 s. That code path was removed.
  • Second-order bounded SA convection ended at residual 0.002501 after 150 updates, versus 0.002379 for the first-order isolation. It is not the recommended fast/robust setting.
  • Further tuning of diffusion ratio bounds is not supported as a primary load-accuracy path because the current change already moves SA substantially without moving the load trajectory.

What this result cancels—and what remains

This result cancels promotion of the SA-only correction as the next solution for CL/CD/CM convergence. It does not invalidate the bounded scalar operator: its dissipative property, CPU/CUDA consistency, monotone residual reduction, and low overhead are useful, so it remains opt-in for later combined tests.

The next high-value numerical target is the procedural-AMR mean-flow DP-LUR operator. It is still scalar and does not carry the five-variable Euler block coupling already available in the explicit-face tetra/hybrid solver. The nearly identical load histories here show that the remaining low-frequency mode is not controlled by the SA scalar solve alone.
